Plus 3, The Jack, and the Kicker

Each add-on changes your cost per draw. Whether it helps depends on the spot you're playing.

Plus 3

Draws 3 extra winning numbers (23 total) and doubles your wager. It's a bad deal on small picks and slightly favorable on 8- and 9-spot, where the bigger pool boosts the rare top prizes.

Kicker

Doubles your wager for a random multiplier (up to 5x) on Club Keno prizes. It breaks even only if the average multiplier is exactly 2. Michigan doesn't publish how often each multiplier comes up, so its true value can't be calculated from the ticket.

Why bet-doubling systems stall

A "cover the pot" system raises the bet after each losing batch so one hit recovers everything. How fast the bet grows depends on one number: the prize multiplier minus the draws per batch. A thin margin makes the bets explode, and the $20-per-draw cap ends every one of these within a few rounds.

Settle before the first ticket

  1. Which ladder, and what counts as "ahead" (any profit, or a set target like +$50).
  2. The top rung is the end of the night. Nobody adds a new stage.
  3. Whose name goes on a claim over $600, and how the group splits it. Big keno wins are taxable income to whoever claims them.
  4. Which bet amounts your retailer allows. Odd amounts like $7, $12, or $15 may need two tickets, and bets over $20 a draw may need several.

Treat the $600 as the price of the night

The ladders here often finish ahead, but the wins are small and the losses are large. Budget the average loss as entertainment, not an investment.
